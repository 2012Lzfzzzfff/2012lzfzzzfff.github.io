About Me

I am a PhD candidate at the University of Southampton, supervised by Dr Ruomeng Huang. My project, Smart Design of Thermoelectric Coolers for On-Chip Cooling Applications, is part of the Marie Skłodowska-Curie doctoral network MGICIAN (Magnesium Alloys for Efficient Solid-State Cooling).

My research sits between thermal engineering and artificial intelligence. Thermoelectric coolers can remove heat from hotspots on chips, but designing one means searching a large space of materials, leg geometries, electrical contacts and package interfaces, and every detailed multiphysics simulation is expensive. I build machine-learning surrogate models that learn from physics-based simulations: neural networks and Gaussian processes combined with physical models of the cooler, so that many designs can be evaluated far faster than with full simulation while the predictions stay within what the physics allows.

I am particularly interested in when such models can be trusted: how much training data they need, how far they can extrapolate beyond the designs they were trained on, how uncertain their predictions are, and how physical knowledge makes them more data-efficient. In the longer term I want to use AI to design and control whole chip cooling systems, with thermoelectric coolers working alongside liquid cooling to reduce the energy the system uses.

Before the PhD I spent two and a half years on thermal and fluid simulation of power electronics at Huawei in Germany, where I built Python and MATLAB workflows for parametric thermal studies, alongside an M.Sc. in Automotive Engineering at the Technical University of Munich.
